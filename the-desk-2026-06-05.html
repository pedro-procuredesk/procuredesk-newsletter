● Operator's Checklist

7 Things to Close Before the Auditor's List Arrives

Audit readiness is built in the 30 to 60 days before fieldwork — not the week before. Run through this list while you still have time to fix what you find.

01Confirm PO dates precede invoice dates. High risk A PO created after an invoice arrives looks like a control bypass to an auditor. Backdate patterns are the most common finding across mid-market AP audits.
02Locate the approval record for every transaction in your likely sample population. High risk Email threads technically qualify, but a system-generated log with approver name and timestamp is what auditors want — and it takes seconds to produce, not hours.
03Pull the receiving documentation for all physical good purchases. High risk A missing goods receipt means you can't prove delivery happened. That's the third leg of 3-way matching, and auditors will ask for it.
04Run a duplicate invoice check before fieldwork begins. Med risk Pull any invoices from the same vendor with the same amount in the same period. A duplicate paid before the auditor notices it is an embarrassing finding that's entirely preventable.
05Audit your vendor master file for ghost vendor indicators. High risk Vendors with no physical address, missing W-9s, or banking details matching an employee account are the exact pattern auditors test for. An annual review catches most of these before they become findings.
06Test your period-end cut-off. Med risk Pull invoices received in the last 5 business days before and after period close. Confirm each was recorded in the right period. Invoices dated in one month but booked in the next are a textbook cut-off finding.
07Reconcile your AP subledger to the general ledger before fieldwork starts. Lower risk Unexplained reconciling items are a direct audit flag. Find them now — not when an auditor points to them first.
